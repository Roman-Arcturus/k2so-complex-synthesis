That design philosophy hits the core of what makes overhaul mods engaging.

In a city-block or Transport Drones setup, single-use items degrade into mindless "stamp and forget" blocks that just add logistics bloat without adding depth. True logistical decision-making only happens when an item or liquid creates **competing priorities and branching paths**:

1. **Multiple Destinations (Demand Competition):** When 3 or 4 completely different production blocks are fighting for the same intermediate (e.g., choosing whether to route a limited supply of `item_rx-wood-chips` toward early circuit substrates, nitrocellulose, or carbon processing), it forces you to prioritize block locations, manage drone traffic, or build dedicated regional sub-assemblies instead of one massive central block.
    
2. **Alternative Recipe Pathways (Supply Choice):** Having two ways to make the same target item—one cheap but dirty/slow, another highly efficient but requiring advanced chemical inputs—gives city-block layouts real purpose. You get to decide whether to upgrade an existing block in-place or construct a high-tech specialized block elsewhere.
    
3. **Byproduct Interdependence:** When a recipe yields a mandatory secondary output, you can't just request inputs and ignore the rest—you have to design overflow handling, local recycling loops, or cross-block balance systems so the primary line never jams.

Yes, the `kr-atmospheric-condenser` can be configured to produce new gases, consume fluid inputs for gas mixing, or require solid items like filters.

Under the hood in Factorio, the `kr-atmospheric-condenser` is built on an `assembling-machine` prototype tied to a specific crafting category (typically `atmosphere-condensation`). Because of this, its capabilities can be expanded in your mod:

### 1. Adding New Extraction Recipes

You can assign any new recipe to its crafting category without changing the machine's model or structure.

- **New Base Outputs:** Carbon Dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$), Argon, atmospheric moisture (Water), or specialized trace gases.
    
- **Byproduct Logistics:** A single condensation recipe can output a primary gas alongside a secondary atmospheric byproduct (for instance, extracting trace Argon while yielding heavy volumes of excess Nitrogen or $\text{CO}_2$). This immediately introduces the storage and handling dilemma you described.
    

### 2. Gas Mixing & Catalyzed Condensation

While standard K2 recipes for the condenser only require electricity and draw from "ambient air," you can modify its prototype `fluid_boxes` in Lua to give it fluid input pipes.

- **Gas Reaction / Scrubbing:** It can take an input gas (or steam/water) and combine it with ambient air extraction to yield synthesized compounds.
    
- **Conditioned Extraction:** It can require a liquid coolant input to condense low-boiling-point gases, forcing a local cooling loop to run the building.
    

### 3. Solid Inputs & Filter Degradation

Because it is an assembling machine, you can also give it item input/output slots.

- **Air Filters:** Require clean filter frames that absorb atmospheric particulates or heavy toxins, returning a dirty filter byproduct that must be cleaned in a separate city-block before returning to the condenser loop.
    
- **Chemical Absorbents:** Require consumable chemical matrices to bind specific rare atmospheric elements.
