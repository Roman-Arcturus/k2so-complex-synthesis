## 1. Early-Game Stone & Sand Balance (Tier 1)

- **The Idle Stone Problem:** In vanilla and standard K2 setups, Stone quickly hits a saturation point after initial brick paving and early belt/furnace crafting, until rails or military science demand it in bulk. Making Sand and Quartz early sinks for Stone creates a continuous baseline load on early mining outposts.
    
- **Preventing the Early Grind:** Increasing Tier 1 material costs is safe as long as the cost scales through **mass/throughput** rather than **time/crafting cycles**. If Sand is cheap, raising the Stone-to-Sand ratio or Quartz-to-Silicon ratio primarily tests belt throughput and mining footprint rather than hand-crafting patience, provided basic crushing/washing can be automated immediately upon unlocking the recipes.
    

## 2. Mid-Game Demand Spike & Tech Placement

- **Technology Timing (`processing-unit` & `production-science-pack`):** This is the ideal inflection point for a material overhaul. At this stage, factory scale shifts from local belt-fed setups to dedicated industrial blocks and train networks. Requiring `processing-unit` guarantees the player already has a working oil/chemical infrastructure to support advanced smelting inputs (such as carbon, acids, or atmospheric gases if applicable).
    
- **The Silicon & Glass Bottleneck:** Tying high-tier electronics directly to massive Quartz and Silicon consumption creates a natural logistics challenge. The player's existing Tier 1 setup will naturally choke on belt density or energy efficiency, cleanly signaling that it is time to dismantle or bypass early smelters in favor of the `industrial-furnace`.
    

## 3. Critical Transition Factors & Risks

To ensure this progression feels rewarding rather than punitive, consider three specific balance mechanics:

### A. The "Bootstrap" Bridge

If Tier 1 is _too_ wasteful or slow, crafting the initial batch of Processing Units and Production Science required to research and build your first array of `industrial-furnace` structures can become a grueling bottleneck.

- **Mitigation:** Ensure Tier 1 throughput is high enough to comfortably feed a small 10–15 SPM setup, even if it burns through Stone patches rapidly. The transition should feel driven by the desire to stop wasting raw ore, not by total deadlock.
    

### B. Defining "Wasteful and Slow" (Tier 1 vs. Industrial Furnace)

There are three ways to make Tier 1 feel obsolete compared to the `industrial-furnace`:

1. **Stoichiometric Yield (Resource Cost):** Tier 1 yields significantly less pure Silicon per raw Quartz input (e.g., direct thermal reduction without reagents leaves behind massive slag/waste), whereas the Industrial Furnace uses chemical agents (e.g., Carbon, Chlorine, or Hydrogen) to achieve near-optimal molar conversion.
    
2. **Footprint & Crafting Speed:** Tier 1 furnaces require large physical arrays and high energy per item produced, making factory expansion geographically footprint-heavy.
    
3. **Byproduct Management:** Tier 1 can either discard byproducts automatically at low yield or produce useless Gravel/Slag, while the Industrial Furnace reclaims these byproducts back into the production loop.
    

### C. Resource Ratios and Material Density

Because Sand is cheap (3 Stone $\rightarrow$ 7–8 Sand), scaling Tier 1 recipes down in yield means raw Sand volume on belts will be extremely high.

- Ensure that Tier 1 glass/silicon processing doesn't bottleneck purely on belt-stacking limits before the player has access to higher-tier belts or bulk material handling. Washing 2 Sand into 1 Quartz is reasonable, but if 18 Quartz only yields 9 Silicon, you are looking at a 4:1 Sand-to-Silicon ratio early on.
